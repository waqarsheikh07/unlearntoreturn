# Why We Unlearn

Most of us were taught religion as a set of rules. Do this. Don't do that. Pray this way. Cover this much. Fear this. Obey that.

But what if the real purpose was never obedience — it was understanding?

## The Problem With "Just Follow"

When someone tells you to "just follow," they're asking you to stop thinking. And when you stop thinking, you become easy to control. A person who doesn't question is a person who can be manipulated — by culture, by politics, by people who use religion as a tool of power.

The Quran itself asks you to reflect. To ponder. To use your mind. The word *tafakkur* (to think deeply) appears over and over. This is not a book that asks for blind faith. It asks for engaged minds.

## What Unlearning Means

Unlearning doesn't mean rejecting what you were taught. It means examining it. Holding it up to the light. Asking: *Is this what the text actually says, or is this what someone told me it says?*

There is a difference between the Quran's message and the layers of cultural interpretation that have built up around it over centuries. Unlearning is the process of separating one from the other.

## Why It Matters Now

We live in a time where information is everywhere but understanding is rare. Where people quote verses without context and use hadith as weapons. Where women, especially, are told what religion means for them — by people who have never asked them what they think.

This program exists because thinking is not a luxury. It is a necessity. And the Quran deserves readers who bring their full minds to it — not just their obedience.

---

*This is what Unlearn to Return is about. Not rebellion. Not rejection. Just honest, fearless thinking.*
